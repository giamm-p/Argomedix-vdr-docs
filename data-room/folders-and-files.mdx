A well-organized folder structure is the foundation of a smooth reviewer experience. When investors, counsel, or counterparties open your data room, the folder hierarchy is the first thing they see — a clear, logical layout helps them find what they need quickly and signals that your team manages diligence with precision. Beyond navigation, folders are also the primary unit for permission management in ArgoMedix VDR: you can grant or restrict access at the folder level, making it straightforward to expose only relevant sections to specific reviewer groups without configuring permissions on every individual file.

## Creating Folders

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the New Folder dialog">
    Click **+ New Folder** in the left sidebar (next to the parent folder where you want the new folder to appear) or click the same button in the main content area toolbar when you have a folder selected. Both options create a folder in the currently active location.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter a folder name">
    Type a descriptive name for the folder. Use a consistent naming convention across your room — numbered prefixes (e.g., `01 –`, `02 –`) keep folders in a predictable order and make the hierarchy easier to scan.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add an optional description">
    Expand the **Description** field to add context for reviewers. The description appears as a tooltip when users hover over the folder name in the sidebar, helping them confirm they're in the right place before opening it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create the folder">
    Click **Create**. The folder appears immediately in the sidebar and main content area, ready for documents or subfolders to be added.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Best Practices for Folder Structure

<Tip>
  Number your top-level folders so they stay in a meaningful order regardless of alphabetical sorting. Use consistent naming patterns (e.g., title case, em-dash separators) so your room looks professional to reviewers from the moment they log in.
</Tip>

Keep folder depth to three levels or fewer when possible. Deeply nested structures force reviewers to click through many layers to reach documents, which slows down diligence. Group documents by functional area rather than by document type alone — reviewers think in terms of topics (financials, IP, HR) not formats (PDFs, spreadsheets).

Below is a suggested folder structure for a typical Series A due diligence room. You can adapt this template to match your deal's specific requirements.

```
Project Falcon – Series A/
├── 01 – Corporate Documents/
│   ├── Certificate of Incorporation
│   └── Cap Table
├── 02 – Financial Statements/
│   ├── P&L Statements
│   └── Balance Sheets
├── 03 – Intellectual Property/
│   └── Patent Filings
├── 04 – Contracts & Agreements/
│   └── Customer Contracts
└── 05 – Team & HR/
    └── Org Chart
```

## Moving and Renaming

Right-click any file or folder in the main content area or sidebar to open the context menu. From there you can:

- **Rename** — edit the name inline. Press **Enter** to confirm or **Escape** to cancel.
- **Move** — open the destination picker and select the target folder. The item and all its contents move instantly.
- **Copy** — create a duplicate in another folder while leaving the original in place. Copied files receive a new activity trail independent of the original.

You can also move files and folders using **drag-and-drop**: click and hold any item in the main content area, drag it over the target folder in the left sidebar until the folder highlights, then release to drop. Drag-and-drop supports moving multiple items at once — hold **Shift** or **Ctrl / ⌘** to select several items before dragging.

## Deleting Files

To delete one or more files, select them in the main content area by clicking the checkbox to the left of each file name, then click the **Delete** button (trash icon) in the toolbar. You can also right-click a single file and choose **Delete** from the context menu.

Deleted files are not immediately removed from your data room. They move to the **Trash**, which is accessible from the bottom of the left sidebar. Files remain in Trash for **30 days**, during which an administrator can restore them to their original location (or a new folder) at any time. After 30 days, files are permanently and irreversibly deleted.

<Warning>
  Deleting a folder removes all documents inside it. Move documents to a new location before deleting a folder you no longer need.
</Warning>
